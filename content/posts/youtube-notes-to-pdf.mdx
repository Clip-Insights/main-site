A PDF is useful because it can leave the browser with you. But exporting every captured frame produces a collection, not necessarily a study guide. The useful work is deciding what a reader, including your future self, needs to understand.

## Choose a small, complete example

Imagine a short lesson about learning by explaining. At 02:15, the speaker suggests explaining an idea without looking. At 04:32, a diagram connects watching, explaining and practising. At 07:06, the lesson asks you to try independently.

You could capture all three moments. The resulting document needs three short explanations, not a transcript of the entire lesson. Our [sample PDF](/sample-notes.pdf) uses original demonstration material so you can inspect a concrete example without an account.

## Capture the image and its meaning

In the extension, select **Snapshot** while the relevant frame is visible. Then add a note explaining why you kept it. “Watch → Explain → Practice” describes the picture; “If I cannot explain the transition, I need to revisit that section” records what you intend to do with it.

If two screenshots show essentially the same thing, remove one before export. If a frame is blurry or obscured, capture it again. The PDF cannot restore information that was never captured clearly.

## Review before downloading

Check the timeline in order. Do the notes make sense without the speaker's voice? Are abbreviations understandable? Is an open question clearly marked as a question, rather than an established fact?

Select **Download**, open the generated PDF, and inspect the result. Check that the images and text you need are present. A successful download is not the same as checking its contents.

## Give the file a future use

Use a name that identifies the topic and purpose, such as “merge-sort-review-notes.pdf.” Place it with the assignment or project where you will need it. A downloads folder full of unnamed exports creates another search problem.

Add a next action to the notes before making the final copy: solve a comparable exercise, explain a diagram, or check an unresolved claim. This gives the next session a starting point.

## Understand download versus upload

A download stores the document on your device. **Upload** sends a generated PDF to your account, subject to file-size and storage limits. Neither action synchronizes every editable note to another browser.

Download a copy for offline study and upload one to keep in your library. For the steps, see [Notes, screenshots & PDF exports](/guides/notes-and-pdf-export).

Before sharing a PDF, check that you are allowed to redistribute any included video frames and that it contains no private information.
