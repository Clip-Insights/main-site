“Check this again” is a familiar note and an unhelpful one. Without a source location or a reason, revisiting it means searching the lesson again. A timestamp narrows the search, but it cannot tell you what you were trying to understand.

## Compare the same note twice

An ordinary note might say: “The order of operations matters.” That is too broad to reconstruct a specific explanation.

A better version is: “04:32 — Filtering before grouping changes which items contribute to the total. Try moving the filter after grouping and compare the result.” The timestamp identifies the moment. The explanation identifies the connection. The action identifies how to test it.

The timestamp is not the entire note; it is the address of the context.

## Use timestamps for local context

They are particularly helpful when the speaker changes a diagram, fixes a bug, introduces an exception or explains why a previous attempt failed. Those moments are easy to lose in a long video and difficult to express with an isolated screenshot.

Capture the moment close to the explanation you need. If you only save the final result, the reasoning may have happened a minute earlier. Replay the relevant part before deciding which location will help your future self.

## Use ordinary notes for synthesis

Not every thought belongs to a single second. A comparison across three examples, a personal plan or a summary of the entire lecture may need a broader note. Do not force a general conclusion to look as though it came from one exact moment.

You can write: “Across the examples, the important difference is when validation happens.” Then list the relevant moments in your own study material. This preserves the difference between the source and your synthesis.

## Keep a question with its reason

Instead of “why?”, write “Why does the function return here instead of continuing to the next check?” Pair it with the moment where the return occurs. Even if you never ask anyone else, the question is now specific enough to investigate.

## Preserve the material beyond the session

Export a PDF when you want a document to review later. Keep the video title and source link, because a timestamp alone is meaningless without a source. Remember that a video can change or disappear; a personal explanation and an appropriately captured frame may still be useful, but they do not give you ownership of the original content.
