A good video note does not need to capture every sentence. It needs to preserve the connection you want to understand later.

This article was first published in 2024 and has been revised for the current extension.

## Keep enough context

A note such as “important” tells you little after the session. Try writing what the speaker showed and why you kept it: “The example checks input before storing it so invalid values never reach the database.”

Attach the timestamp while the explanation is fresh. If a diagram matters, use **Snapshot** to keep the current video frame. The screenshot and the written explanation serve different purposes.

## Distinguish a fact from a question

Write an unresolved question plainly: “Why is this case handled separately?” Do not turn a guess into a confident rule just to make the notes look complete.

After a short section, look away from the lesson and try to explain the idea. The point where you get stuck gives you a precise reason to revisit the video.

## Keep a usable export

Review the timeline before selecting **Download**. Remove duplicates, clarify abbreviations and check the resulting PDF. Local notes remain in your browser; a downloaded file is a separate copy.

If you sign in and select **Upload**, the generated PDF can be managed in the web app. That upload does not automatically copy your editable timeline to another browser.

## Use the next session to test yourself

Leave one concrete task, such as applying the idea to a different example. Start with that task next time before replaying the whole lesson.
