A study session does not become useful simply because it produces more notes. Try these three changes to make the material you keep easier to use.

This article was first published in 2024 and has been revised for the current product.

## 1. Give the session a question

Before pressing play, write what you are trying to understand. Choose something narrower than the title of the entire course: “Why does this example validate input before saving?” is a question you can investigate during a short section.

Use the question to decide which moments deserve a note. You do not have to preserve every slide to keep the reasoning that matters.

## 2. Capture a frame and explain it

Use **Snapshot** for a diagram, code example or visual step worth keeping. Add a timestamped note explaining the connection in your own words.

If you cannot explain it yet, record the uncertainty. A clear question is more useful than an unsupported conclusion. AI summaries and chat can help you explore available transcript text, but check important details against the original video.

## 3. Leave a next action

Finish by choosing a small task you can attempt without the instructor. Apply the method to another example, reconstruct a diagram or test a boundary case in code.

Download a PDF of the material you want to review and open it once to check the contents. Local notes live in your browser; a PDF download provides a separate copy. Uploading a PDF is optional and does not synchronize the entire editable timeline.

Next time, begin with the task before replaying the lesson. The difficulty you encounter will tell you what to revisit.
