A summary can help you identify a video's structure. It can also omit the exception, caveat or visual example that makes the explanation correct. The useful question is not whether a summary sounds fluent; it is whether it supports the task you are doing.

## Know what the system receives

Clip Insights uses available transcript text for its AI features. A spoken explanation may be represented in the captions, while an on-screen formula or code change is not. A transcript can mishear a technical term or omit punctuation that changes its meaning.

Long videos may also exceed the text processed in a request. You should not assume every second contributed equally to a response. The [AI guide](/guides/ai-and-transcripts) explains these limits.

## Read the overview as a map

Use the summary to identify the main topics and decide where to focus. If you are learning why an algorithm works, an overview of its steps is only the beginning. Return to the section that explains the invariant, failure case or tradeoff.

An efficient next action is specific: “I understand the steps, but not why this comparison is safe.” That gives you a question to ask and a section to inspect.

## Check one important claim

Choose the claim most relevant to your goal and compare it with the original video. Is the speaker making a general rule, an example-specific observation or a tentative suggestion? A summary that removes those distinctions may sound clearer while becoming less accurate.

Suppose an answer says a method “always uses less memory.” Look for the actual comparison. Does it depend on input size, representation or a particular implementation? Keep those conditions in your own note.

## Ask for the missing connection

Use video chat to explore a defined question. “How does the second example differ from the first?” invites a checkable comparison. “Tell me everything important” gives you another broad summary to evaluate.

Do not interpret an answer as a citation merely because it mentions the video. Verify the relevant segment yourself. If the claim is important beyond the lesson, consult an authoritative independent source too.

## Preserve uncertainty

If you cannot verify an explanation, keep it as an unresolved question. It is better to write “check whether this holds for empty input” than to store an unsupported rule as settled knowledge.

For personal, medical, financial or legal decisions, a transcript-based study assistant is not a substitute for qualified advice. Avoid putting sensitive information into prompts.

Your final notes should show what you checked, what you understood and what you still need to investigate. The AI can help you work; it should not make those distinctions disappear.
